Short interest vs price

Two panels on one shared time axis rather than one chart with two y-scales — a dual axis lets you manufacture any correlation you like by rescaling. Read them together: price falling while short interest climbs is shorts winning; price rising while short interest falls is covering, and that is what a squeeze looks like as it starts.

What to buy — squeeze candidates

Ranked by a score built from four things: how much short interest is trapped, how many days of average volume it would take to cover it, whether shorts are actually covering into strength right now, and whether the macro backdrop is helping. Every argument is spelled out — a score you cannot argue with is a score you should not trade.

What to short

Deliberately conservative. Anything already above 8% short interest is excluded no matter how weak it looks — joining a crowded short is how you end up on the wrong side of the squeeze you were trying to find.

Short-interest extremes in your book

Sudden changes in who is betting against something you own. A sharp build means someone with a research budget took a view against you — worth finding out what they saw. A sharp unwind is often the early part of a re-rate.

Worth researching now

Stocks across the ASX 300 where the short position moved — built or unwound — rather than merely sitting high. A high level is already the whole of the first tab; a change is new information. These are the leads: go read the announcements and work out what the shorts know.

Track record

The engine replayed over stored history: at each past date it was scored using only the data available on that day, then measured against what the price actually did next. Everything is shown as excess return — the bucket's average minus the average of every stock over the same windows — because in a rising market every signal looks clever until you subtract the market.
